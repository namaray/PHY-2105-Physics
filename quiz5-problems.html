SECTION 1: Slide Problems

Slide Problem-1 — Electron lifted 520 m against the field Slide

Problem: An electron is moved upward 520 m by the electric force, in a downward field of 150 N/C. Find the change in its electric potential energy and in the electric potential.

📝 Solution
\[ W = q\vec E\cdot\vec d = qEd\cos180^\circ = (-1.6\times10^{-19})(150)(520)(-1) = 1.25\times10^{-14}\ \text{J} \]
\[ \Delta U = -W = -1.2\times10^{-14}\ \text{J},\qquad \Delta V = \frac{\Delta U}{q} = \frac{-1.2\times10^{-14}}{-1.6\times10^{-19}} = 7.5\times10^{4}\ \text{V} \]
The slide prints ΔV = 4.5×10⁴ V (45 kV) — that's an arithmetic slip: 1.2/1.6 = 0.75, so it's 7.5×10⁴ V. Unrounded it is exactly \(Ed = 150\times520 = 7.8\times10^4\) V. Show the division line so your working stands either way.
Answer: \(\Delta U = -1.2\times10^{-14}\) J (energy falls); \(\Delta V = +7.5\times10^4\) V — the electron moves to a higher potential.

Slide Problem-2 — Potential at the centre of a square Slide

Problem: \(q_1 = 12\) nC, \(q_2 = -24\) nC, \(q_3 = 31\) nC, \(q_4 = 17\) nC at the corners of a square of side \(d = 1.3\) m. What is \(V\) at the centre P?

📝 Solution

Potential is a scalar: no components, just add with signs. Each corner is \(r = d/\sqrt2 = 0.919\) m away.

\[ V = \frac{k(q_1+q_2+q_3+q_4)}{r} = \frac{(8.99\times10^9)(36\times10^{-9})}{0.919} = 352\ \text{V} \]
Answer: ≈ 350 V (the slide's answer).

Slide Problem-3 — Field AND potential at the origin Slide

Problem: −1.0 μC at (0, 1) and +1.0 μC at (1, 0). At the origin find E (magnitude, direction), V, and the energy to bring +1.0 μC there from infinity.

📝 Solution
\[ E_{each} = \frac{k(10^{-6})}{1^2} = 9000\ \text{N/C};\quad E = \sqrt2\,(9000) = 1.27\times10^{4}\ \text{N/C at }135^\circ \]
\[ V = 9000 - 9000 = 0\ \text{V},\qquad W = qV = 0 \]
Answer: \(E = 1.27\times10^4\) N/C at 135° (up and left); \(V = 0\); no work needed. Key idea: E can be non-zero where V is zero.

Slide Problem-4 — Proton and alpha: closest approach Slide

Problem: A proton (m, +e) and an alpha (4m, +2e) approach each other with the same speed v. How close do they get?

📝 Solution
\[ \frac{k(e)(2e)}{r} = \tfrac12mv^2 + \tfrac12(4m)v^2\ \Longrightarrow\ r = \frac{4ke^2}{5mv^2} \]
This is the slide's method (all kinetic energy becomes potential energy). Strictly, momentum is conserved so they can't both stop; the exact answer is \(5ke^2/4mv^2\). Use the slide's version in the exam.
Answer: \(r = 4ke^2/(5mv^2)\).

Slide Problem-6 — Potential energy of three charges Slide

Problem: \(q_1 = +q\), \(q_2 = -4q\), \(q_3 = +2q\) at the corners of an equilateral triangle, \(d = 12\) cm, \(q = 150\) nC. Find U.

📝 Solution

Energy belongs to each pair; add all three pairs:

\[ U = \frac{k}{d}\left[(q)(-4q) + (q)(2q) + (-4q)(2q)\right] = -\frac{10kq^2}{d} = -\frac{10(8.99\times10^9)(150\times10^{-9})^2}{0.12} \]
Answer: \(U = -1.7\times10^{-2}\) J = −17 mJ. Negative: an external agent would have to do 17 mJ of work to pull the system apart.

SECTION 2: Practice Problem Sheet-5

P1 — Square: +2q, +4q, +2q, −2q Practice

Problem: Side 10 cm, \(q = 57\times10^{-9}\) C. Find V at the intersection of the diagonals.

📝 Solution
\[ V = \frac{k(2+4+2-2)q}{0.1/\sqrt2} = \frac{(8.99\times10^9)(6)(57\times10^{-9})}{0.0707} = 4.35\times10^{4}\ \text{V} \]
Answer: \(4.35\times10^4\) V. (Compare PPS-4 #6: the field there needed vectors; potential just adds.)

P2 — Potential and work Practice

Problem: (i) V at 0.09 m from \(4\times10^{-7}\) C; (ii) work to bring \(2\times10^{-9}\) C from infinity.

📝 Solution
\[ V = \frac{(8.99\times10^9)(4\times10^{-7})}{0.09} = 4.0\times10^{4}\ \text{V},\qquad W = qV = (2\times10^{-9})(4.0\times10^4) = 8.0\times10^{-5}\ \text{J} \]
Answer: (i) \(4.0\times10^4\) V; (ii) \(8.0\times10^{-5}\) J.

P3 — Thunderstorm in electron-volts Practice

Problem: \(\Delta V = 1.2\times10^9\) V between ground and cloud. Change in an electron's potential energy, in eV?

📝 Solution
\[ |\Delta U| = e|\Delta V| = (1\ e)(1.2\times10^9\ \text{V}) = 1.2\times10^{9}\ \text{eV} = 1.2\ \text{GeV} \]
Answer: \(1.2\times10^9\) eV. (1 eV = \(1.6\times10^{-19}\) J — charge in units of e times volts gives eV directly.)

P4 — Two −q charges, potential at P Practice

Problem: Two charges of \(10\times10^{-5}\) C (shown as −q) at \((\pm0.10, 0)\) m; P at (0, 0.10) m. Find |V| at P.

📝 Solution
\[ V = 2\cdot\frac{k(-1\times10^{-4})}{\sqrt{0.02}} = -1.27\times10^{7}\ \text{V} \]
Answer: \(|V| = 1.27\times10^7\) V (negative charges give negative V).

P5 — Potential from the field Practice

Problem: E = 5.0 N/C at 60 cm from a charge. Find V and q.

📝 Solution
\[ q = \frac{Er^2}{k} = 2.0\times10^{-10}\ \text{C},\qquad V = \frac{kq}{r} = Er = (5.0)(0.60) = 3.0\ \text{V} \]
Answer: V = 3.0 V, q = 0.20 nC. Shortcut for a point charge: V = Er.

P6 — Ionosphere voltage Practice

Problem: Fair-weather field 100 N/C over 100 km. Voltage?

📝 Solution
\[ V = Ed = (100)(100\times10^{3}) = 1.0\times10^{7}\ \text{V} \]
Answer: \(1.0\times10^7\) V (10 MV).

P7 — Ammonia dipole Practice

Problem: NH₃: p = 1.47 D (1 D = \(3.34\times10^{-30}\) C·m). V at 52.0 nm along the dipole axis.

📝 Solution
\[ p = 4.91\times10^{-30}\ \text{C·m},\qquad V = \frac{kp\cos0^\circ}{r^2} = \frac{(8.99\times10^9)(4.91\times10^{-30})}{(52.0\times10^{-9})^2} = 1.63\times10^{-5}\ \text{V} \]
Answer: \(1.63\times10^{-5}\) V.

P8 — Proton and alpha, v = 2×10⁸ m/s On the syllabus

Problem: Same as slide Problem-4 with \(v = 2\times10^8\) m/s. How close do they get?

📝 Solution
\[ r = \frac{4ke^2}{5m_pv^2} = \frac{4(8.99\times10^9)(1.6\times10^{-19})^2}{5(1.67\times10^{-27})(2\times10^8)^2} = 2.76\times10^{-18}\ \text{m} \]
Slide method. The strict (momentum-conserving) answer is \(5ke^2/4m_pv^2 = 4.31\times10^{-18}\) m — mention it only if asked.
Answer: \(2.76\times10^{-18}\) m.

P9 — Alpha particle stopped by gold Practice

Problem: Alpha (2e) stops at r = 9.23 fm from a gold nucleus (79e). Initial kinetic energy?

📝 Solution
\[ K_i = \frac{k(2e)(79e)}{r} = \frac{(8.99\times10^9)(158)(1.6\times10^{-19})^2}{9.23\times10^{-15}} = 3.94\times10^{-12}\ \text{J} = 24.6\ \text{MeV} \]
Answer: \(3.94\times10^{-12}\) J ≈ 24.6 MeV.

P10 — Potential at the origin On the syllabus

Problem: −1.0 μC at (0, 1) and +3.0 μC at (3, 0). (i) V at origin; (ii) energy to bring +3.0 μC there.

📝 Solution
\[ V = k\left(\frac{-1\times10^{-6}}{1} + \frac{3\times10^{-6}}{3}\right) = 0,\qquad W = qV = 0 \]
Answer: (i) 0 V; (ii) 0 J — the bigger charge is three times further away, so the two contributions cancel exactly.

P11 — Three charges, potential at P Practice

Problem: \(q_1 = q_2 = +e\), \(q_3 = +2e\), \(a = 6.00\ \mu\)m (same figure as PPS-4 #13).

📝 Solution
\[ r = \frac{a}{\sqrt2},\qquad V = \frac{k(e+e+2e)}{a/\sqrt2} = \frac{4\sqrt2\,ke}{a} = 1.36\times10^{-3}\ \text{V} \]
Answer: \(1.36\times10^{-3}\) V. (The field from 1 and 2 cancelled at P; their potentials don't.)

P12 — Charges on an arc On the syllabus

Problem: 3 protons and 2 electrons on an arc of radius 2.00 cm. |V| at the centre?

📝 Solution
\[ V = \frac{ke(3-2)}{r} = \frac{(8.99\times10^9)(1.6\times10^{-19})}{0.020} = 7.19\times10^{-8}\ \text{V} \]
Answer: \(7.19\times10^{-8}\) V. The angles are irrelevant — potential is a scalar and every charge is at the same distance.

P13 — Work along a field line Practice

Problem: An electron moves A → B; the field does \(3.94\times10^{-19}\) J of work. Find \(V_B-V_A\), \(V_C-V_A\), \(V_C-V_B\).

📝 Solution
\[ W = -q\Delta V\ \Longrightarrow\ V_B - V_A = -\frac{W}{q} = -\frac{3.94\times10^{-19}}{-1.6\times10^{-19}} = +2.46\ \text{V} \]

B and C lie on the same equipotential, so \(V_C = V_B\).

The sheet says “two electron”, almost certainly a typo for “an electron”. If it really is charge −2e, halve the answers: 1.23 V.
Answer: (i) +2.46 V; (ii) +2.46 V; (iii) 0.

P14 — Work to bring Q near two charges Practice

Problem: Q = +16e from infinity to a point 2.00d from \(q_1 = +4e\) and d from \(q_2 = -q_1/3\); d = 1.40 cm.

📝 Solution

Work depends only on the end point (the angles and path don't matter):

\[ W = QV = 16e\cdot k\left(\frac{4e}{2d} - \frac{4e/3}{d}\right) = \frac{32ke^2}{3d} = 1.75\times10^{-25}\ \text{J} \]
Answer: \(1.75\times10^{-25}\) J.

P15 — Four charges, potential at P Practice

Problem: q = 5.00 fC, d = 4.00 cm, arrangement as shown.

📝 Solution

Distances from P: +q at d, −q at d, −q at 2d, +q at d.

\[ V = kq\left(\frac1d - \frac1d - \frac1{2d} + \frac1d\right) = \frac{kq}{2d} = 5.62\times10^{-4}\ \text{V} \]
Answer: \(5.62\times10^{-4}\) V.

P17 — Rectangular array On the syllabus

Problem: a = 39.0 cm, \(q_1 = 3.40\) pC, \(q_2 = 6.00\) pC, charges as shown. V at the centre?

📝 Solution

The four corners are all the same distance from the centre and their charges add to 2 − 3 − 1 + 2 = 0, so they contribute nothing. The two middle charges (4q₂ each) are a/2 away:

\[ V = \frac{k(8q_2)}{a/2} = \frac{16kq_2}{a} = \frac{16(8.99\times10^9)(6.00\times10^{-12})}{0.390} = 2.21\ \text{V} \]
Answer: 2.21 V.

P18 — Protons and electrons around a centre On the syllabus

Problem: 4 protons at 0.6 cm and 4 electrons at 0.1 cm from the centre (PPS-4 #25 figure). V at the centre, and energy to bring +2.2 μC there.

📝 Solution
\[ V = 4ke\left(\frac{1}{0.006} - \frac{1}{0.001}\right) = -4.80\times10^{-6}\ \text{V} \]
\[ U = qV = (2.2\times10^{-6})(-4.80\times10^{-6}) = -1.06\times10^{-11}\ \text{J} \]
Answer: \(V = -4.80\times10^{-6}\) V; \(U = -1.06\times10^{-11}\) J. In PPS-4 the field here was zero by symmetry — the potential isn't, because scalars don't cancel by direction.
