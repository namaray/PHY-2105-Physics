1. SHM Definition & Restoring Force

Simple Harmonic Motion occurs when the restoring force is directly proportional to displacement from equilibrium and directed toward the equilibrium position:

\[ F = -kx \]

where \( k \) is the spring constant (N/m) and \( x \) is the displacement. The negative sign indicates the force is always directed opposite to displacement (toward equilibrium).

Characteristics of SHM:

  • Motion is periodic (repeats in time)
  • Restoring force is proportional to displacement
  • Acceleration is always directed toward equilibrium
  • Motion is sinusoidal in nature

2. Differential Equation of SHM May appear in Quiz

From Newton's Second Law \( F = ma \) and Hooke's Law \( F = -kx \):

\[ ma = -kx \] \[ m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -kx \] \[ \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \omega_0^2 x = 0 \]

This is the standard differential equation of SHM, where \( \omega_0^2 = \frac{k}{m} \).

3. General Solution of SHM

The solution to the differential equation gives displacement, velocity, and acceleration as functions of time:

\[ x(t) = A\cos(\omega_0 t + \delta) \] \[ v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt} = -A\omega_0 \sin(\omega_0 t + \delta) \] \[ a(t) = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -A\omega_0^2 \cos(\omega_0 t + \delta) = -\omega_0^2 x(t) \]

where:

  • \( A \) = Amplitude (maximum displacement)
  • \( \omega_0 \) = Angular frequency (rad/s)
  • \( \delta \) (or \( \phi \)) = Phase constant / initial phase
  • \( (\omega_0 t + \delta) \) = Phase of the motion

4. Complete SHM Parameters Critical for Quiz

Angular Frequency:

\[ \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \frac{2\pi}{T} = 2\pi f \]

Time Period:

\[ T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_0} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} \]

Frequency:

\[ f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{\omega_0}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \]

Velocity at position x:

\[ v(x) = \pm\omega_0\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} \]

5. Maximum & Minimum Parameters Critical for Quiz

Parameter Maximum Minimum Occurs When
Displacement \( x_{max} = A \) \( x_{min} = 0 \) Max at extreme, Min at equilibrium
Velocity \( v_{max} = A\omega_0 \) \( v_{min} = 0 \) Max at equilibrium (x=0), Min at extremes (x=±A)
Acceleration \( a_{max} = A\omega_0^2 \) \( a_{min} = 0 \) Max at extremes (x=±A), Min at equilibrium (x=0)
Total Energy \( E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \) (constant) Always constant, never changes

Key relationships at any position \( x \):

\[ v = \omega_0\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} \quad \Rightarrow \quad v_{max} = A\omega_0 \text{ (at } x = 0\text{)} \] \[ a = -\omega_0^2 x \quad \Rightarrow \quad |a_{max}| = \omega_0^2 A \text{ (at } x = \pm A\text{)} \]

6. Proof: \( E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \) from Sinusoidal Equation Important Proof

Step 1: Write KE and PE using sinusoidal SHM equations:

\[ x = A\cos(\omega_0 t + \delta), \quad v = -A\omega_0\sin(\omega_0 t + \delta) \]

Step 2: Kinetic Energy:

\[ K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m A^2\omega_0^2 \sin^2(\omega_0 t + \delta) \]

Since \( \omega_0^2 = \frac{k}{m} \), we get \( m\omega_0^2 = k \), so:

\[ K = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \sin^2(\omega_0 t + \delta) \]

Step 3: Potential Energy:

\[ U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \cos^2(\omega_0 t + \delta) \]

Step 4: Total Energy:

\[ E = K + U = \frac{1}{2}kA^2\left[\sin^2(\omega_0 t + \delta) + \cos^2(\omega_0 t + \delta)\right] \]

Since \( \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1 \):

\[ \boxed{E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2} \]

✅ Total energy is constant and depends only on amplitude — independent of time!

7. Proof: Time Period is Constant — \( y(t) = y(t + T) \) Important Proof

We need to show that if \( x(t) = A\cos(\omega_0 t + \delta) \), then \( x(t+T) = x(t) \).

\[ x(t + T) = A\cos[\omega_0(t + T) + \delta] \] \[ = A\cos[\omega_0 t + \omega_0 T + \delta] \]

Since \( \omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T} \), we have \( \omega_0 T = 2\pi \):

\[ x(t + T) = A\cos[\omega_0 t + 2\pi + \delta] \] \[ = A\cos[\omega_0 t + \delta] = x(t) \]

✅ Since \( \cos(\theta + 2\pi) = \cos\theta \), the motion repeats after every period \( T \). Hence proved!

8. SHM Energy Graphs Graph Drawing

Energy vs Displacement Graph:

  • KE curve: \( K = \frac{1}{2}k(A^2 - x^2) \) — inverted parabola, max at \( x = 0 \), zero at \( x = \pm A \)
  • PE curve: \( U = \frac{1}{2}kx^2 \) — upward parabola, zero at \( x = 0 \), max at \( x = \pm A \)
  • Total E: Horizontal straight line at \( E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \)
  • KE and PE curves intersect at \( x = \pm \frac{A}{\sqrt{2}} \) where \( K = U = \frac{E}{2} \)

Energy vs Time Graph:

  • KE: \( K = \frac{1}{2}kA^2\sin^2(\omega_0 t + \delta) \) — oscillates between 0 and \( \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \) with frequency \( 2\omega_0 \)
  • PE: \( U = \frac{1}{2}kA^2\cos^2(\omega_0 t + \delta) \) — oscillates between 0 and \( \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \) with frequency \( 2\omega_0 \)
  • Total E: Constant horizontal line at \( E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \)
  • KE and PE are always complementary: when one is max, the other is zero

9. SHM Wave Graphs: x-t, v-t, a-t Graph Drawing

Displacement vs Time (\( x \) vs \( t \)):

  • Cosine wave: \( x(t) = A\cos(\omega_0 t + \delta) \)
  • Oscillates between \( +A \) and \( -A \)

Velocity vs Time (\( v \) vs \( t \)):

  • Negative sine wave: \( v(t) = -A\omega_0\sin(\omega_0 t + \delta) \)
  • Leads displacement by \( \frac{\pi}{2} \) (90°)
  • Oscillates between \( +A\omega_0 \) and \( -A\omega_0 \)

Acceleration vs Time (\( a \) vs \( t \)):

  • Negative cosine: \( a(t) = -A\omega_0^2\cos(\omega_0 t + \delta) \)
  • Leads displacement by \( \pi \) (180°), i.e., opposite phase
  • Oscillates between \( +A\omega_0^2 \) and \( -A\omega_0^2 \)

📌 Key: All three graphs have the same period T but different amplitudes and phase relationships.
