📐 Notation used throughout

\( \alpha = \dfrac{b}{2m} \) is the damping factor (some books write \( \gamma \) — same number). \( \omega_0 \) is the natural angular frequency, \( \omega_d \) the damped one, and \( \omega \) a driving frequency when one is present.

📋 A. Formula Sheet

A1 — Simple Harmonic Motion

1. SHM equation

\[ \ddot{x} + \omega_0^2x = 0 \]

2. Solution

\[ x = A\cos(\omega_0t + \phi) \]

3. Natural frequency

\[ \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}},\quad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} \]

4. Velocity & acceleration

\[ v_{max} = A\omega_0,\quad a_{max} = A\omega_0^2 \]

and \( a = -\omega_0^2x \) at any instant.

5. Energy

\[ E = \tfrac{1}{2}kA^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}m\omega_0^2A^2 \]

Constant. \(K\) and \(U\) each average \( \tfrac{1}{4}kA^2 \).

6. Velocity at position x

\[ v(x) = \pm\omega_0\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} \]

7. Simple pendulum

\[ \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}},\quad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \]

8. Compound pendulum

\[ \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{mgL}{I}},\quad T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I}{mgL}} \]

Reduces to #7 when \( I = mL^2 \).

A2 — Damped Harmonic Motion

9. DHM equation

\[ m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = 0 \]

i.e. \( \ddot{x} + 2\alpha\dot{x} + \omega_0^2x = 0 \)

10. Damping factor

\[ \alpha = \frac{b}{2m} \]

11. Underdamped solution

\[ x = A_0e^{-\alpha t}\cos(\omega_dt + \phi) \]

12. Damped frequency

\[ \omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - \alpha^2},\quad T_d = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d} \]

13. Damping conditions

\[ \alpha < \omega_0 : \text{under} \\ \alpha = \omega_0 : \text{critical} \\ \alpha > \omega_0 : \text{over} \]

Equivalently \( b^2 \) vs \( 4mk \).

14. Critical damping

\[ b_c = 2m\omega_0 = 2\sqrt{mk} \]

15. Amplitude & energy decay

\[ A(t) = A_0e^{-\alpha t},\quad E(t) = E_0e^{-2\alpha t} \]

Energy decays at twice the amplitude rate.

16. Relaxation time (lifetime)

\[ \tau = \frac{m}{b} = \frac{1}{2\alpha} \]

Energy definition — see the warning below.

17. Logarithmic decrement

\[ \delta = \ln\frac{A_n}{A_{n+1}} = \alpha T_d \]

How you get \( \alpha \) off a graph, then \( b = 2m\alpha \).

18. Damping force

\[ F_d = -b\,v \]

⚠️ Two decay times — the classic trap

Amplitude and energy do not decay at the same rate, so "falls to \(1/e\)" has two answers:

what decayslawreaches \(1/e\) athalves at
amplitude\(A_0e^{-\alpha t}\)\(1/\alpha = 2m/b\)\(\ln 2/\alpha\)
energy ← #16\(E_0e^{-2\alpha t}\)\(1/2\alpha = m/b\)\(\ln 2/2\alpha\)

Your slides define lifetime by the energy, so use \( \tau = m/b \) unless the question explicitly says amplitude.

A3 — Driven Oscillations & Resonance

19. Driven equation

\[ m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + kx = F_0\sin\omega t \]

\( \omega \) is the driving frequency, not \( \omega_0 \).

20. Steady-state amplitude

\[ A_0 = \frac{F_0}{m\sqrt{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2 + \left(\frac{b\omega}{m}\right)^2}} \]

21. Amplitude resonance

\[ \omega_r = \sqrt{\omega_0^2 - 2\alpha^2} \]

Slightly below \( \omega_0 \); no peak at all if \( 2\alpha^2 > \omega_0^2 \).

22. Velocity resonance

\[ \omega = \omega_0 \]

Exactly at \( \omega_0 \), unshifted by damping.

23. Quality factor

\[ Q = \frac{m\omega_0}{b} = \frac{\omega_0}{2\alpha} \]

Large \(Q\) → sharp peak. Small \(Q\) → broad peak.

24. Phase lag

\[ \tan\phi_0 = \frac{b\omega}{m(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)} \]

A4 — LC and RLC Circuits

25. LC circuit

\[ \ddot{Q} + \frac{1}{LC}Q = 0 \]

Electrical SHM — no damping at all.

26. LC frequency

\[ \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}},\quad T = 2\pi\sqrt{LC} \]

27. RLC equation

\[ \frac{d^2Q}{dt^2} + \frac{R}{L}\frac{dQ}{dt} + \frac{1}{LC}Q = 0 \]

28. Series \( \alpha \) and \( \omega_0 \)

\[ \alpha = \frac{R}{2L},\quad \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} \]

29. Series: oscillatory?

\[ R < 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} \]

If \( R > R_c \) → overdamped, no oscillation.

30. Series: critical R

\[ R_c = 2\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} \]

31. Resonant frequency

\[ f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}} \]

Exists even when the circuit is overdamped.

32. Series: frequency of oscillation

\[ f_d = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \left(\frac{R}{2L}\right)^2} \]

33. Parallel: oscillatory?

\[ R > \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} \]

Inequality flips. Parallel needs a large R.

34. Parallel: \( \alpha \) and critical R

\[ \alpha = \frac{1}{2RC},\quad R_c = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} \]

\( \alpha \) uses \( 1/2RC \) here, not \( R/2L \).

35. Parallel: frequency of oscillation

\[ f_d = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{1}{LC} - \frac{1}{4R^2C^2}} \]

36. Charge decay

\[ Q(t) = Q_0e^{-\frac{R}{2L}t}\cos(\omega_dt) \]

⚡ Series vs. parallel — the one thing to remember

Both share \( \omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC} \). Only the damping term changes: series \( \alpha = R/2L \), parallel \( \alpha = 1/2RC \). Everything else follows from \( \omega_d = \sqrt{\omega_0^2-\alpha^2} \), and it oscillates whenever \( \alpha < \omega_0 \).

Because \( R \) is on top in series but on the bottom in parallel, the condition reverses, and the critical values differ by a factor of 4.

A5 — Wave Equation

37. Progressive wave

\[ y = A\sin(\omega t - kx) \]

\( -kx \) → travels \( +x \); \( +kx \) → travels \( -x \).

38. Alternative forms

\[ y = A\sin 2\pi\left(\frac{t}{T}-\frac{x}{\lambda}\right) = A\sin\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}(vt-x) \]

39. Wave number & speed

\[ k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda},\quad v = \frac{\omega}{k} = f\lambda \]

40. Differential wave equation

\[ \frac{\partial^2y}{\partial t^2} = v^2\frac{\partial^2y}{\partial x^2} \]

41. Particle velocity

\[ \frac{\partial y}{\partial t} = -v\frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \]

\( v_{particle,max} = A\omega \) — unrelated to the wave speed.

42. Phase difference

\[ \Delta\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta x = \frac{2\pi}{T}\Delta t \]

43. Standing wave

\[ y = 2A\sin(\omega t)\cos(kx) \]

Nodes at \( \lambda/4, 3\lambda/4,\ldots \); antinodes at \( 0, \lambda/2,\ldots \)

44. Lissajous figures

\[ x = A_1\cos\omega t,\ \ y = A_2\cos(\omega t+\phi) \]

\( \phi=0 \) → line; \( \phi=\pi/2 \) → ellipse (circle if \( A_1=A_2 \)).

⚡ D. Last-Minute Checks

Traps that cost easy marks

  • \( \tau = m/b \), not \( 2m/b \) — energy definition (formula #16).
  • Parallel RLC flips the inequality and uses \( \alpha = 1/2RC \) (#33, #34).
  • Particle speed \( A\omega \) has nothing to do with wave speed \( f\lambda \) (#41).
  • Overdamped is slower than critical, not faster.
  • Convert units first: gm → kg, gm/s → kg/s, μF and mH → F and H, dyne/cm → N/m (\( 65000\ \text{dyne/cm} = 65\ \text{N/m} \)).
  • Graph starting at a peak → cosine; starting at zero → sine.
  • \( \omega_d < \omega_0 \) always, so the damped period is longer.
