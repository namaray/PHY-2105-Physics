⚡ Derivations — Exam Version

PHY-2105 Finals • each derivation in a few lines, most-asked first

Write exactly this in the exam: draw the diagram, write the Use line, then the lines below it, then box the result. The red badge says how many of the 13 past finals asked it. Stuck on a line? The full version explains every step.

1. Dipole field on the axis 8/13 papers

Use: \( \displaystyle E = \frac{kq}{r^2},\ \ p = qd \)

\( \displaystyle E = \frac{kq}{(z - \frac d2)^2} - \frac{kq}{(z + \frac d2)^2} \)
\( \displaystyle = kq\,\frac{2zd}{(z^2 - \frac{d^2}{4})^2} \)
\( \displaystyle \approx kq\,\frac{2zd}{z^4} = \frac{2kqd}{z^3}\qquad (z \gg d) \)
\( \displaystyle E = \frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{p}{z^3} \)

Along the axis, same direction as p.

Draw this

2. Dipole potential 7/13 papers

Use: \( \displaystyle V = \frac{kq}{r} \)

\( \displaystyle V = \frac{kq}{r_+} - \frac{kq}{r_-} = kq\,\frac{r_- - r_+}{r_-\,r_+} \)
\( \displaystyle r_- - r_+ \approx d\cos\theta,\qquad r_-\,r_+ \approx r^2\qquad (r \gg d) \)
\( \displaystyle V = \frac{kqd\cos\theta}{r^2} \)
\( \displaystyle V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{p\cos\theta}{r^2} \)

Axis (θ = 0): kp/r². Bisector (θ = 90°): 0.

Draw this

3. Torque on a dipole 5/13 papers

Use: \( \displaystyle F = qE,\ \ \tau = F\times\text{lever arm} \)

\( \displaystyle \text{forces } +qE \text{ and } -qE\ \Rightarrow\ F_{net} = 0 \)
\( \displaystyle \text{lever arm of each} = \tfrac d2\sin\theta \)
\( \displaystyle \tau = 2\,(qE)\,\tfrac d2\sin\theta = qEd\sin\theta = pE\sin\theta \)
\( \displaystyle \vec\tau = \vec p\times\vec E \)

Max pE at 90°. Zero at 0° and 180°.

Draw this

4. Dipole potential energy 5/13 papers

Use: \( \displaystyle \tau = pE\sin\theta,\ \ \Delta U = -W \)

\( \displaystyle W = \int_{\theta_0}^{\theta}(-pE\sin\theta)\,d\theta = pE(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0) \)
\( \displaystyle U - U_0 = -W = -pE(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0) \)
\( \displaystyle \theta_0 = 90^\circ,\ U_0 = 0\ \Rightarrow\ U = -pE\cos\theta \)
\( \displaystyle U = -pE\cos\theta = -\vec p\cdot\vec E \)

Max +pE at 180°. Zero at 90°. Min −pE at 0°.

Draw this

5. Potential of a point charge 5/13 papers

Use: \( \displaystyle V_f - V_i = -\int\vec E\cdot d\vec s,\ \ E = \frac{kq}{r^2} \)

\( \displaystyle 0 - V = -\int_R^{\infty}\frac{kq}{r^2}\,dr \)
\( \displaystyle = -kq\left[-\frac1r\right]_R^{\infty} = -\frac{kq}{R} \)
\( \displaystyle \text{group: } V = \sum\frac{kq_i}{r_i} \)
\( \displaystyle V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r} \)

Same sign as q. Zero at infinity.

Draw this

6. Field of a charged ring 4/13 papers

Use: \( \displaystyle dE = \frac{k\,dq}{r^2},\ \ r^2 = z^2 + R^2,\ \ \cos\theta = \frac zr \)

\( \displaystyle \text{sideways parts cancel (symmetry)} \Rightarrow \text{keep } dE\cos\theta \)
\( \displaystyle dE\cos\theta = \frac{k\,dq}{z^2+R^2}\cdot\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+R^2}} = \frac{kz\,dq}{(z^2+R^2)^{3/2}} \)
\( \displaystyle E = \frac{kz}{(z^2+R^2)^{3/2}}\int dq \)
\( \displaystyle E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{qz}{(z^2+R^2)^{3/2}} \)

z = 0: E = 0. z ≫ R: E ≈ kq/z².

Draw this

7. V from the field 3/13 papers

Use: \( \displaystyle dW = \vec F\cdot d\vec s,\ \ F = q_0E,\ \ \Delta V = -\frac{W}{q_0} \)

\( \displaystyle dW = q_0\,\vec E\cdot d\vec s \)
\( \displaystyle W = q_0\int_i^f\vec E\cdot d\vec s \)
\( \displaystyle V_f - V_i = -\frac{W}{q_0}\qquad(\text{definition of } \Delta V) \)
\( \displaystyle = -\frac{q_0\int_i^f\vec E\cdot d\vec s}{q_0}\qquad(\text{put in } W;\ q_0 \text{ cancels}) \)
\( \displaystyle V_f - V_i = -\int_i^f\vec E\cdot d\vec s \)

Uniform field: ΔV = −Ed.

Draw this

8. Potential of a rod (length L) 3/13 papers

Use: \( \displaystyle dV = \frac{k\,dq}{r},\ \ dq = \lambda\,dx,\ \ r = \sqrt{x^2+d^2} \)

\( \displaystyle V = k\lambda\int_0^L\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+d^2}} \)
\( \displaystyle = k\lambda\Big[\ln\big(x + \sqrt{x^2+d^2}\big)\Big]_0^L \)
\( \displaystyle V = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\left[\frac{L + \sqrt{L^2+d^2}}{d}\right] \)

Bottom limit gives ln d; ln a − ln b = ln(a/b).

Draw this

9. Field of a point charge 1/13 papers

Use: \( \displaystyle F = \frac{kqq_0}{r^2},\ \ E = \frac{F}{q_0} \)

\( \displaystyle \vec E = \frac{1}{q_0}\cdot\frac{kqq_0}{r^2}\,\hat r \)
\( \displaystyle \vec E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}\,\hat r \)

Away from +q, toward −q.

Draw this

10. Energy of a system of charges slides

Use: \( \displaystyle W = qV,\ \ V = \frac{kq}{r} \)

\( \displaystyle \text{bring } q_1:\ W = 0;\quad \text{bring } q_2:\ W = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r_{12}} \)
\( \displaystyle \text{bring } q_3:\ W = \frac{kq_1q_3}{r_{13}} + \frac{kq_2q_3}{r_{23}} \)
\( \displaystyle U = \sum_{\text{pairs}}\frac{kq_iq_j}{r_{ij}} \)

One term per pair; keep the signs.

Draw this

11. Field from potential slides

Use: \( \displaystyle dW = q_0E\cos\theta\,ds,\ \ dW = -q_0\,dV \)

\( \displaystyle q_0E\cos\theta\,ds = -q_0\,dV \)
\( \displaystyle E_s = -\frac{dV}{ds} \)

E points where V falls fastest.

Draw this

12. Potential of any continuous charge slides

Use: \( \displaystyle dV = \frac{k\,dq}{r} \)

\( \displaystyle V = \int dV \)
\( \displaystyle V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\int\frac{dq}{r} \)

dq = λ ds (line), σ dA (surface), ρ dV (volume).
