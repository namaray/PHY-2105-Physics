1. Charge & Coulomb's Law — notes: coulombs-law.html

Coulomb's law

\[ F = \frac{k|q_1q_2|}{r^2} \]

Size from the formula; direction from the signs.

Vector form

\[ \vec F_{12} = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r^2}\,\hat r_{12} \]

r̂ points from the source to the charge acted on.

Superposition

\[ \vec F_{net} = \vec F_1 + \vec F_2 + \cdots \]

Add forces as vectors — tip to tail.

Components & resultant

\[ F = \sqrt{F_x^2+F_y^2},\ \ \theta = \tan^{-1}\frac{F_y}{F_x} \]

Check the quadrant from the signs of Fx, Fy.

Quantization

\[ q = ne \]

Charge comes in whole multiples of e.

Charge in a sample

\[ q = \frac{m}{M}N_A\,Z\,e \]

Grams → moles → atoms → electrons → coulombs.

Charged spheres

\[ F = \frac{kq_1q_2}{(r+2R)^2} \]

If r is the gap, add both radii.

Electric vs gravity

\[ \frac{F_e}{F_g} = \frac{kq_1q_2}{Gm_1m_2} \]

The r² cancels — the ratio is distance-free.

Newton's 2nd law

\[ a = \frac{F}{m} \]

"Acceleration as they separate".

Current

\[ I = \frac{dq}{dt},\quad Q = \int i\,dt \]

Charge = area under the i–t graph.

Voltage

\[ V = \frac{dW}{dq} \]

Energy per coulomb.

Power

\[ P = VI = \frac{dW}{dt},\quad W = \int P\,dt \]

Energy = area under the P–t graph.

2. Electric Field — notes: electric-fields.html

Definition

\[ \vec E = \frac{\vec F}{q_0} \]

N/C. Direction = force on a positive test charge.

Point charge

\[ E = \frac{k|q|}{r^2} \]

Away from +, toward −.

Force from a field

\[ \vec F = q\vec E \]

Negative charges feel a force opposite to E.

Superposition

\[ \vec E = \sum \vec E_i \]

Each field from its own charge, then vector-add.

Two equal fields at angle φ

\[ E_{net} = 2E\cos\frac{\varphi}{2} \]

60° → √3E, 90° → √2E, 120° → E.

Null point

\[ \frac{q_1}{x^2} = \frac{q_2}{(d-x)^2} \]

Square-root both sides; no quadratic.

Charge in a uniform field

\[ a = \frac{|q|E}{m} \]

Constant sideways force → parabola.

Line-charge element

\[ dq = \lambda\,ds,\quad dE = \frac{k\,dq}{r^2} \]

Treat each element as a point charge.

Charged ring (axis)

\[ E = \frac{kqz}{(z^2+R^2)^{3/2}} \]

Zero at the centre; → kq/z² far away.

Dipole moment

\[ p = qd \]

Points from − to +. Unit C·m.

Dipole field (axis, far)

\[ E = \frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{p}{z^3} = \frac{2kp}{z^3} \]

Bisector: kp/r³, opposite to p.

Torque on a dipole

\[ \tau = pE\sin\theta,\quad \vec\tau = \vec p\times\vec E \]

Max pE at 90°, zero at 0° and 180°.

Dipole potential energy

\[ U = -pE\cos\theta = -\vec p\cdot\vec E \]

Lowest when aligned (stable).

Work rotating a dipole

\[ W_{agent} = U_f - U_i = -W_{field} \]

0°→180° costs 2pE.

Cross product

\[ \vec a\times\vec b = \begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\a_x&a_y&a_z\\b_x&b_y&b_z\end{vmatrix} \]

Don't drop the minus on the ĵ term.

Field-line rules

\[ \text{line density} \propto E \]

Tangent = direction of E; lines never cross.

3. Electric Potential — notes: electric-potential.html

Potential energy change

\[ \Delta U = U_f - U_i = -W \]

Field does + work → U falls.

Potential

\[ V = \frac{U}{q} \]

Volt = J/C. A property of the point.

Potential difference

\[ \Delta V = \frac{\Delta U}{q} = -\frac{W}{q} \]

Keep the sign of q (electron: q = −e).

Work to bring q from ∞

\[ W_{ext} = qV \]

Path doesn't matter.

From the field

\[ V_f - V_i = -\int_i^f \vec E\cdot d\vec s \]

V falls along E.

Uniform field

\[ \Delta V = -Ed \]

Equipotentials: evenly spaced planes.

Point charge

\[ V = \frac{kq}{r} \]

1/r, not 1/r² — and keeps the sign.

Group of charges

\[ V = \sum \frac{kq_i}{r_i} \]

Scalars: add with signs.

Equal distances

\[ V = \frac{k\sum q_i}{r} \]

Square centre, arc centre, ring axis.

Point-charge shortcut

\[ V = Er \]

From E = kq/r² and V = kq/r.

Dipole potential (far)

\[ V = \frac{kp\cos\theta}{r^2} \]

Zero everywhere on the bisector.

Continuous charge

\[ V = \int \frac{k\,dq}{r} \]

Still a scalar sum.

Gradient

\[ E_s = -\frac{dV}{ds} \]

E points where V falls fastest.

Pair energy

\[ U = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r} \]

Like +, unlike −.

System energy

\[ U = \sum_{pairs}\frac{kq_iq_j}{r_{ij}} \]

3 charges → 3 pairs; 4 → 6.

Closest approach

\[ K_{initial} = \frac{kq_1q_2}{r_{min}} \]

Two movers: add both K's (slide method).

Electron-volt

\[ 1\ \text{eV} = 1.6\times10^{-19}\ \text{J} \]

ΔU in eV = (q/e) × ΔV.

Energy conservation

\[ K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f \]

Speed from a drop: ½mv² = |qΔV|.

Equipotentials

\[ \Delta V = 0 \Rightarrow W = 0 \]

Always ⊥ to field lines; no work along them.

4. Geometry Shortcuts

Square

\[ r = \frac{a}{\sqrt2} \]

Corner to centre.

Rectangle 2a × a

\[ r = \frac{\sqrt5}{2}a \]

Corner to centre; middle of long side: a/2.

Equilateral triangle

\[ h = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}a,\quad r_G = \frac{a}{\sqrt3} \]

Vertex to centroid.

Isosceles right triangle

\[ \text{hyp} = a\sqrt2 \]

Midpoint of hypotenuse is a/√2 from all corners.

Point on the bisector

\[ r = \sqrt{d^2+y^2} \]

Resolve with cos θ = d/r, sin θ = y/r.

Handy triangles

\[ 3\text{-}4\text{-}5,\quad 1\text{-}2\text{-}\sqrt5 \]

Exact cosines without a calculator.
