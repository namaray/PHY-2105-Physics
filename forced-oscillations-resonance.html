Forced Oscillations & Resonance

Waves & Oscillations • Part I

1. Driven Harmonic Motion

Forced vibrations occur when there is a periodic driving force applied to a system. This force may or may not have the same period as the natural frequency of the system.

Consider what happens when we apply a time-dependent force \( F(t) \) to a system that normally would carry out SHM with an angular frequency \( \omega_0 \).

Assume the external force is \( F(t) = F_0 \cos(\omega t) \). The equation of motion becomes:

\[ ma = -kx - bv + F_0 \cos(\omega t) \] \[ m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + b\frac{dx}{dt} + kx = F_0 \cos(\omega t) \]

The steady state motion of this system will be harmonic motion with an angular frequency equal to the angular frequency of the driving force.

2. Steady State Solution

The general solution takes the form:

\[ x(t) = A_0 \sin(\omega t + \phi_0) \]

Where the amplitude \( A_0 \) depends on the driving frequency \( \omega \), natural frequency \( \omega_0 \), and damping constant \( b \):

\[ A_0 = \frac{F_0}{m \sqrt{(\omega^2 - \omega_0^2)^2 + \left(\frac{b\omega}{m}\right)^2}} \]

3. Resonance

If the driving frequency is close to the natural frequency of the system (\( \omega \approx \omega_0 \)), the resulting amplitudes can be very large even for small driving amplitudes. The system is said to be in resonance.

In realistic systems, there will always be a damping force. Whether or not resonance behavior will be observed depends on the strength of the damping term. The sharpness of the resonant peak depends on the damping:

  • If the damping is small, the peak can be quite sharp.
  • If the damping is large, the peak is broad and less sharp.

Q Factor

The width of the resonant peak can be characterized by the Q factor (Quality factor):

\[ Q = \frac{m\omega_0}{b} \]

4. Real World Examples

Like damping, resonance can be wanted or unwanted:

  • Unwanted Resonance: Large-amplitude oscillations of the Tacoma Narrows Bridge, due to gusty winds, led to its collapse in 1940. Collapse of a freeway in California due to the 1989 earthquake.
  • Wanted Resonance: Musical instruments and TV/radio receivers depend on resonance to amplify specific frequencies and tune into specific channels.
