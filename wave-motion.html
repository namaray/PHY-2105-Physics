Wave Motion

Waves & Oscillations • Part I

1. Types of Waves

A wave is a traveling disturbance that transports energy from place to place. There are two basic types of waves:

  • Transverse: The medium oscillates perpendicular to the direction the wave is moving (e.g., water waves, electromagnetic light waves).
  • Longitudinal: The medium oscillates in the same direction as the wave is moving (e.g., sound waves moving air back and forth).

2. Wave Properties

  • Wavelength (λ): The distance between identical points on the wave.
  • Period (T): The time for a point on the wave to undergo one complete oscillation.
  • Speed (v): The wave moves one wavelength λ in one period T, so its speed is v = λ/T = fλ.

Note: The speed of a wave is a constant that depends only on the medium. The speed of a wave particle is NOT the same as the speed of the wave; the particle performs simple harmonic motion with velocity v = Aω cos(ωt).

3. Equation for a Progressive Wave

The simplest type of wave is one in which the particles of the medium are set into simple harmonic vibrations as the wave passes through it. The displacement of a particle at a distance x traveling left to right with velocity v is:

y = A sin( ωt - φ )
Phase difference φ = (2πx) / λ

Substituting this phase difference yields the progressive wave equation:

y = A sin( 2π(t/T - x/λ) )
y = A sin[ (2π/λ)(vt - x) ]

4. Differential Equation for Wave Motion

Differentiating the progressive wave equation y = A sin[ (2π/λ)(vt - x) ] twice with respect to x and twice with respect to t gives us:

d2y/dt2 = -A(4π2v2/λ2) sin[ (2π/λ)(vt - x) ]
d2y/dx2 = -A(4π2/λ2) sin[ (2π/λ)(vt - x) ]

Comparing these equations gives the general differential equation of wave velocity:

d2y/dt2 = v2 (d2y/dx2)

5. Superposition & Lissajous' Figures

When a particle is influenced simultaneously by two simple harmonic motions at right angles to each other, the resultant motion traces a curve called a Lissajous' figure. The shape depends on the time period, phase difference, and amplitude of the constituent vibrations.

x = A1 cos(ωt)
y = A2 cos(ωt + φ)
  • If φ = 0, the path is a straight line (Rectilinear motion).
  • If φ = π/2, the path is an ellipse (x2/A12 + y2/A22 = 1).

6. Stationary (Standing) Waves

A stationary wave is produced by the superposition of two progressive waves of the same amplitude and frequency but traveling in opposite directions.

  • Nodes (N): Points that remain stationary (no displacement occurs).
  • Antinodes (A): Points of largest amplitude of oscillation.

Unlike progressive waves, no energy is transferred along the direction of propagation in a stationary wave, and the wave profile does not move.
