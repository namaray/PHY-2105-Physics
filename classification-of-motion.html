Classification of Motion

Waves & Oscillations • PHY-2105

1. Introduction to Motion

Motion can be broadly classified into two main categories: Periodic and Aperiodic motion. From these primary types, we can further classify motion into several specific types based on the path and behavior of the particle.

  • Periodic Motion: Includes Linear, Circular/Rotational, Oscillatory/Vibrational, and Rota-Vibratory motion.
  • Aperiodic Motion: Motion that does not repeat at regular intervals.

2. Periodic Motion

Definition: When a particle moves from one place to another at a set interval of time, that motion is called Periodic Motion.

The most common mathematical representation of a periodic motion is the Sine Wave, where the displacement of a particle repeats in a cyclic manner over specific intervals (e.g., 0, π, 2π, etc.).

3. Linear & Circular Motion

Linear Motion: When a particle is moving in a straight path, the motion is linear motion.
Examples: A car moving from left to right in a straight path, the average motion of a train.

Circular/Rotational Motion: When a particle is moving in a circular path, the motion is said to be Circular or Rotational Motion.
Examples: Motion of the hands of a clock, motion of a wheel.

4. Oscillatory & Rota-Vibratory Motion

Oscillatory/Vibrational Motion: When particles are moving to and fro or up and down towards a mean point, then the motion is said to be Oscillatory/Vibrational Motion.
Examples: Motion of a simple pendulum, motion of a tuning fork.

Rota-Vibratory/Rotational-Vibrational Motion: When the particles are moving under the influence of both rotational and circular motion, the overall motion is called Rota-Vibratory/Rotational-Vibrational Motion.
Examples: Motion of an electric motor, Earth's rotation towards the Sun.

5. Aperiodic Motion

Definition: When particles are moving in an indefinite interval of time (not repeating perfectly), it is called Aperiodic Motion.

An aperiodic wave typically decays or changes its amplitude over time rather than maintaining a constant cycle.

Examples: Damped Harmonic Motion (DHM) where the amplitude decreases over time, a tornado.
