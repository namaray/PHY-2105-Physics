Simple Harmonic Motion & Energy

Waves & Oscillations • Part I

1. Parameters of Periodic Motion

Many kinds of motion repeat over and over, such as the vibrations of a quartz crystal in a watch, a swinging pendulum, and the back-and-forth motion of a piston. This is called periodic motion.

  • Amplitude (A): The maximum magnitude of displacement from the equilibrium position. It is always positive.
  • Period (T): The time required for one complete oscillation.
  • Frequency (f): The number of cycles in a unit time.

2. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

The simplest kind of oscillation occurs when the restoring force \( F_x \) is directly proportional to the displacement from equilibrium \( x \).

\[ F_x = -kx \]

Using Newton's Second Law, the differential equation for a mass-spring oscillator is:

\[ \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = -\frac{k}{m}x \]

General Solution: Guessing a trial solution yields:

\[ x(t) = A \cos(\omega_0 t + \phi) \] \[ v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt} = -A\omega_0 \sin(\omega_0 t + \phi) \] \[ a(t) = \frac{dv}{dt} = -A\omega_0^2 \cos(\omega_0 t + \phi) = -\omega_0^2 x(t) \]

Where the natural angular frequency \( \omega_0 \) and Period \( T \) are:

\[ \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \] \[ T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} \]

The phase difference between velocity and displacement is \( \frac{\pi}{2} \), and between acceleration and displacement is \( \pi \).

3. Energy Approach

Assuming no dissipative mechanisms (no friction), the total energy of the mass-spring system is conserved.

  • Kinetic Energy (K): \( \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \)
  • Potential Energy (U): \( \frac{1}{2}kx^2 \)
\[ E = K + U = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 + \frac{1}{2}kx^2 = \text{Constant} \] \[ E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \cos^2(\omega_0 t + \phi) + \frac{1}{2}mA^2\omega_0^2 \sin^2(\omega_0 t + \phi) \] \[ E = \frac{1}{2}kA^2 \]

From energy conservation, we can find velocity at any position x:

\[ v(x) = \pm \omega_0 \sqrt{A^2 - x^2} \]

4. Pendulums

The General Pendulum: For a displaced pendulum, the gravitational potential energy is \( mgL(1 - \cos\theta) \). For small angles, \( \cos\theta \approx 1 - \frac{\theta^2}{2} \).

Applying the conservation of energy with moment of inertia \( I \):

\[ \text{Total Energy} = \frac{1}{2}I\left(\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{2}mgL\theta^2 = \text{constant} \] \[ \frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} = -\frac{mgL}{I}\theta \]

This is the SHM equation with \( \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{mgL}{I}} \).


The Simple Pendulum: A particle of mass \( m \) attached to a cable of length \( L \) of negligible mass. The restoring force is \( F = -mg \sin\theta \approx -mg\theta \).

\[ \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}} \] \[ T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \]
