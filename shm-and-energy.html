Simple Harmonic Motion & Energy

Waves & Oscillations • Part I

1. Parameters of Periodic Motion

Many kinds of motion repeat over and over, such as the vibrations of a quartz crystal in a watch, a swinging pendulum, and the back-and-forth motion of a piston. This is called periodic motion.

  • Amplitude (A): The maximum magnitude of displacement from the equilibrium position. It is always positive.
  • Period (T): The time required for one complete oscillation.
  • Frequency (f): The number of cycles in a unit time.

2. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

The simplest kind of oscillation occurs when the restoring force Fx is directly proportional to the displacement from equilibrium x.

Fx = -kx

Using Newton's Second Law, the differential equation for a mass-spring oscillator is:

d2x / dt2 = -(k/m)x

General Solution: Guessing a trial solution yields:

x(t) = A cos(ω0t + φ)

v(t) = dx/dt = -Aω0 sin(ω0t + φ)
a(t) = dv/dt = -Aω02 cos(ω0t + φ) = -ω02x(t)

Where the natural angular frequency ω0 and Period T are:

ω0 = √(k/m)
T = 2π√(m/k)

The phase difference between velocity and displacement is π/2, and between acceleration and displacement is π.

3. Energy Approach

Assuming no dissipative mechanisms (no friction), the total energy of the mass-spring system is conserved.

  • Kinetic Energy (K): ½mv2
  • Potential Energy (U): ½kx2
Total Energy (E) = ½mv2 + ½kx2 = Constant

E = ½kA2 cos2(ω0t + φ) + ½mA2ω02 sin2(ω0t + φ)
E = ½kA2

From energy conservation, we can find velocity at any position x:

v(x) = ±ω0√(A2 - x2)

4. Pendulums

The General Pendulum: For a displaced pendulum, the gravitational potential energy is mgL(1 - cosθ). For small angles, cosθ ≈ 1 - θ2/2.

Applying the conservation of energy with moment of inertia I:

Total Energy = ½I(dθ/dt)2 + ½mgLθ2 = constant
d2θ/dt2 = -(mgL/I)θ

This is the SHM equation with ω0 = √(mgL/I).


The Simple Pendulum: A particle of mass m attached to a cable of length L of negligible mass. The restoring force is F = -mg sinθ ≈ -mgθ.

ω0 = √(g/L)
T = 2π√(L/g)
