LC Circuits

Waves & Oscillations • Part I

1. Introduction to LC Circuits

An LC circuit, also called a resonant circuit, tank circuit, or tuned circuit, consists of an inductor, represented by the letter \( L \), and a capacitor, represented by the letter \( C \). When connected together, they can act as an electrical resonator.

During the charging and discharging of an LC Circuit, the energy shifts between the electric field of the capacitor and the magnetic field of the inductor.

2. Equations of the LC Circuit

Using Kirchhoff's voltage law, the sum of the voltages across the components must be zero:

\[ V_C = \frac{Q}{C} \quad \text{(Voltage across capacitor)} \] \[ V_L = L\frac{di}{dt} \quad \text{(Voltage across inductor)} \] \[ \frac{Q}{C} + L\frac{di}{dt} = 0 \]

Since current \( i = \frac{dQ}{dt} \), we can rewrite the equation in terms of current:

\[ \frac{d^2i}{dt^2} + \frac{1}{LC}i = 0 \]

This is mathematically identical to the differential equation of Simple Harmonic Motion.

\[ \text{SHM: } \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + \omega_0^2x = 0 \]

3. Frequency and Time Period

By comparing the LC circuit differential equation to the standard SHM equation, we find the natural angular frequency \( \omega_0 \):

\[ \omega_0^2 = \frac{1}{LC} \implies \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} \] \[ T = 2\pi\sqrt{LC} \] \[ f = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}} \]

The solutions for charge and current are oscillatory:

\[ Q(t) = Q_0 \cos(\omega_0 t + \phi) \] \[ i(t) = -i_0 \sin(\omega_0 t + \phi) \]

4. Mechanical vs Electrical Analogy

The mathematical forms of mechanical oscillators and electrical LC circuits correspond directly to one another:

  • Displacement (x) corresponds to Charge (Q)
  • Velocity (v) corresponds to Current (i)
  • Mass (m) corresponds to Inductance (L)
  • Spring constant (k) corresponds to 1/Capacitance (1/C)

Energy Analogy:

  • Potential energy \( (\frac{1}{2}kx^2) \) corresponds to Electric energy in capacitor \( (\frac{1}{2}\frac{Q^2}{C}) \)
  • Kinetic energy \( (\frac{1}{2}mv^2) \) corresponds to Magnetic energy in inductor \( (\frac{1}{2}Li^2) \)
