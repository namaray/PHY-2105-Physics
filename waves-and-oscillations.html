Waves & Oscillations

Complete Notes • PHY-2105

1. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

The simplest kind of oscillation occurs when the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement from equilibrium. The equation of motion is:

F = -kx

In SHM, the object periodically moves back and forth between amplitudes x = +A and x = -A. Key parameters include:

  • Amplitude (A): Maximum displacement from equilibrium.
  • Period (T): Time required for one complete oscillation.
  • Frequency (f): Number of cycles per unit time (f = 1/T).
  • Angular Frequency (ω): Related to period by ω = 2π/T.

Common examples of SHM include the simple pendulum and the spring-mass system.

2. Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion

In an ideal SHM system with no friction, the total mechanical energy is conserved.

  • Potential Energy (U): U = ½kx²
  • Kinetic Energy (K): K = ½mv²
  • Total Energy (E): E = U + K = ½kA² (constant)

The maximum kinetic energy occurs at the equilibrium position (x=0), while the maximum potential energy occurs at maximum displacement (x=±A).

3. Damped & Forced Oscillations

Damped Oscillations: In real systems, frictional forces dissipate energy. This causes the amplitude of oscillation to decrease over time and eventually die out. The damping force is often proportional to the velocity (F' = -bv).

Forced Oscillations & Resonance: When a periodic driving force is applied to an oscillating system, forced vibrations occur. If the frequency of this driving force matches the natural frequency of the system, the amplitude becomes extremely large. This phenomenon is called Resonance.

4. Wave Motion & Properties

A wave is a traveling disturbance that transports energy from place to place. There are two primary types of waves:

  • Transverse Waves: The medium oscillates perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation (e.g., water waves, light waves).
  • Longitudinal Waves: The medium oscillates parallel to the direction of wave propagation (e.g., sound waves).

The speed of a wave (v) is constant in a given medium and relates wavelength (λ) and period (T) by the equation v = λ / T = fλ.

5. Superposition & Standing Waves

Stationary (Standing) Waves: Produced by the superposition of two progressive waves of the same amplitude and frequency traveling in opposite directions (e.g., a wave reflecting off a wall).

  • Nodes: Points where no displacement occurs (destructive interference).
  • Antinodes: Points where the largest amplitude of oscillation occurs (constructive interference).

Lissajous' Figures: When a particle is influenced simultaneously by two simple harmonic motions at right angles to each other, the resultant path traces a curve known as a Lissajous figure. The shape depends on the phase difference and frequency ratio of the constituent vibrations.
